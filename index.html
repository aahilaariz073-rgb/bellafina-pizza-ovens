Why a Pizza Oven

The One Appliance People Stand Around

A grill is something the host disappears to. A pizza oven works the other way — it cooks fast enough that people stay beside it, stretch their own dough, watch a pizza go in and eat it standing up. That is most of the reason to buy one.

It also does something an indoor kitchen cannot. The floor temperature a dedicated oven reaches is what produces a cooked base and a leopard-spotted crust; a domestic oven does not get there. When the pizzas are finished, the retained heat is still good for bread, roasted vegetables, whole fish or a slow-cooked shoulder.

The oven that gets used every other week is the one that heats fast enough for a weeknight. Everything else is a party appliance.

Which is why the first question we ask is how often you expect to cook. That answer points to a fuel, a size and an installation type faster than any spec sheet does.

Gas Pizza Ovens

Gas: Hot Enough on a Tuesday

The argument for gas is frequency. It reaches cooking temperature quickly, holds it without anyone tending a fire, and shuts off when you are done — which is what turns a pizza oven from a weekend event into a weeknight one. Temperature control is steady enough that the first pizza and the tenth come out the same.

Gas ovens are also easier to build into a kitchen run, since they run off the same gas line as the grill, and the chimney is simpler than a wood-fired oven needs.

  • Ready in 15–20 minutes, holds its own temperature. Best if you want pizza on a weeknight without babysitting a fire.
  • Runs on natural gas or propane. Best if a gas line already feeds your grill — no new fuel source, no wood to store.
  • Built-in or freestanding, in most sizes. Best if the oven has to fit a finished patio or an island you are still designing.
  • Dual-fuel models burn either. Best if one of you wants the ritual of wood and the other wants a button.

Wood-Fired Pizza Ovens

Wood: Live Fire, Smoke and the Ritual

A wood-fired oven asks more of you and gives back something gas cannot: the smell, the flame licking the dome, and a smoke character that carries into everything cooked after the pizzas are done. Building and managing the fire is the appeal for the people who want one, and a chore for the people who don't.

Heavier masonry ovens take longer to come up to temperature but hold heat for hours afterward, which suits a long evening that starts with pizza and finishes with bread. The practical items are somewhere to keep the wood, where the chimney runs, and where the smoke ends up relative to windows and neighbors.

  • Real flame and wood-smoke flavor. Best if tending the fire is part of the fun, not a chore you want to skip.
  • Two builds: heavy masonry or lighter insulated dome. Masonry takes 1–2 hours to heat but stays hot all evening — best for long dinners that end with bread. An insulated dome is ready in about 30 minutes and cools faster — best for a quicker night.
  • Needs a chimney route and safe spacing from anything that can burn. Best sorted before the surround is built — we work it out for you, and your builder gets it in writing.
  • Dual-fuel models burn wood or gas. Best if you want wood on Saturday and no cleanup on Wednesday.

How to Choose an Outdoor Pizza Oven

Five questions decide which oven suits your backyard. Answer them before anything gets built and the rest is straightforward — we can walk you through all five in one conversation.

Gas or Wood
Ask yourself how often you will really cook. Once a week or more → gas: it is hot in 15–20 minutes and shuts off when you are done. A few times a season, and you enjoy the process → wood: real flame, smoke flavor, about an hour to heat and a fire to tend. Want both → dual-fuel.
Built-In or Freestanding
Ask yourself whether anything is being built. Building or remodeling an outdoor kitchen → built-in: it looks part of the run and sits at a comfortable counter height, but it needs a base strong enough to carry it, safe spacing around it and a gas line run in. Patio already finished, or you may move house → freestanding: it arrives on a cart or stand, needs no construction, and goes with you.
Size — How Many Pizzas at Once
Ask yourself how many people you usually feed. Family of four → a single-pizza floor is plenty and heats fastest. Regular parties of ten or more → size up so two pizzas go in at once, or you will be cooking all night. In a wood oven, a bigger floor also leaves room to push the fire to one side.
How Long It Takes to Get Hot
You trade one for the other. Want to decide at 6pm that it is pizza night → a lighter insulated oven, hot in roughly 20–30 minutes, cools just as fast. Want to keep cooking after the pizzas — bread, a roast, vegetables → a heavier masonry oven, slower to heat but still hot hours later. Tell us which evening you are picturing and we will match the model to it.
Where the Smoke Goes
Ask yourself what sits above the oven, and how close the neighbors are. Every oven comes with a required chimney route and a minimum safe distance from anything that can burn — a real constraint under a solid roof, pergola or covered patio. Wood makes far more smoke, so on a tight lot or near windows, gas is often the easier answer. This is the one item worth settling first, and we handle the numbers for you.
Pizza Oven Questions

Common Questions About Outdoor Pizza Ovens

Gas or wood-fired pizza oven — which should I get?

Gas gets to temperature in a fraction of the time and holds it without tending, which in practice means you use it on a Tuesday and not just at a planned party. Wood gives you live fire, the smoke character and the ritual, at the cost of a longer heat-up and a fire to manage while you cook. Dual-fuel models split the difference. Be honest about how often you will cook: the oven you use most is the one worth buying.

Built-in or freestanding pizza oven?

A built-in oven drops into an island or a masonry enclosure and becomes part of the kitchen run — it looks integrated, it sits at a fixed working height, and it needs its opening, its safe spacing and its gas line designed in from the start. A freestanding oven arrives on a cart or a stand, needs no construction, and can be moved or taken with you. If the outdoor kitchen is being built anyway, built-in is usually the better result; if it already exists, freestanding is far less disruptive.

How hot does an outdoor pizza oven get, and how long does it take?

Neapolitan-style pizza is generally cooked at very high floor temperatures, which is why a dedicated oven produces a result a domestic kitchen oven cannot. Heat-up time varies enormously by design: compact gas ovens reach cooking temperature quickly, while heavier masonry ovens take considerably longer but hold heat far better afterward. Manufacturers publish figures for each model, and we can compare them against how you plan to cook.

Can a pizza oven be built into an existing outdoor kitchen?

Sometimes, but it is more involved than swapping a grill. A built-in oven needs a base strong enough to carry it, the manufacturer's safe distance from anything that can burn, somewhere for the smoke to go, and a gas line if it runs on gas — and a wood-fired one adds a chimney that has to vent somewhere safe. Retrofitting into an existing island usually means opening up part of the run. Where that is impractical, a countertop or freestanding model gets you cooking without the construction.

Does a pizza oven need a hood or a chimney?

Every oven has a chimney or vent set by its manufacturer, and the smoke has to leave safely — which is a live question when the oven sits under a solid roof, a pergola or a covered patio. Wood-fired ovens produce far more smoke and need more thought about where the chimney runs and where that smoke ends up relative to windows and neighboring properties. Settle this at design stage; it is not a finish detail.

What does an outdoor pizza oven cost?

The range is broad. A compact freestanding gas oven is a single purchase. A built-in oven in a custom masonry enclosure adds the base, the veneer, the chimney, the gas work and the labor, and lands considerably higher. Tell us whether you are leaning built-in or freestanding, gas or wood, and we can give you a realistic figure to plan against.
